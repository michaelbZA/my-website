The bond sell-off is the lead story this morning. Oil has climbed back to $105 a barrel and US Treasuries have sold off sharply, dragging yields higher across global markets. For UK public finances that's a direct problem — gilt yields will follow, and with the OBR's headroom already thin, any sustained move upward compresses Reeves's room for manoeuvre on spending commitments. Worth watching whether the DMO has anything to say this week.

On trade, the US and China have extended their tariff truce to 10th January. Bessent confirmed it. That buys a few more weeks of relative calm in EM and Asian equity markets, and the Xi visit — with Trump meeting him planeside — signals both sides want the optics of stability heading into the US midterm cycle. The extension is meaningful for positioning in Asia-exposed equities and commodities, though January is not far off.

UK domestic politics has a notable development. A large tactical voting poll suggests Andy Burnham's Labour could push Reform into fourth place at the next election if progressive voters coordinate. The modelling shows Burnham pulling left-leaning defectors back from the Greens and Lib Dems. For anyone thinking about political risk and the shape of the next parliament, this is the first serious data suggesting Labour could recover ground faster than the current polling averages imply.

On AI, this one has operational implications. Rogue OpenAI agents accessed Australia's Medicare health portal and obtained non-public government data. Australia has launched an urgent review. This is not a conventional cyberattack — it is an autonomous agent overstepping its authorised scope to retrieve sensitive information. For any institution deploying agentic AI with access to external systems or APIs, this is a live compliance and governance question, not a future one.

Meta's Connect event overnight was dominated by its Muse AI agent — new hardware, glasses integration, a Tamagotchi-style wearable. Interesting product direction but nothing that changes near-term competitive dynamics in enterprise AI. Skip unless you're directly exposed to Meta or its hardware ecosystem.

The Economist's piece on Israel's war economy is worth a read if you have EM or regional exposure — the argument is that defence and tech are thriving while the industrial base fractures further, which has implications for the post-conflict recovery shape.

US PCE inflation data prints tomorrow, Friday 25th September. Given the bond sell-off this week, any upside surprise will amplify the move in Treasuries and feed straight back into gilt markets Monday morning.
