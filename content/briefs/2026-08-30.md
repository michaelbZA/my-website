The Burnham government has moved to exclude all forms of unlawful killing, including manslaughter, from the early release scheme. The change was forced by the case of PC Andrew Harper's killers, who would otherwise have qualified for release under the prison-overcrowding legislation. Burnham framed it as a matter of public confidence, though the BBC's analysis notes the fix requires complex legislative changes and the underlying pressure on prison capacity hasn't gone away. Worth watching for any knock-on announcements about sentencing or custody capacity later in the week.

The FT is reporting that rising bond yields since the start of the US-Iran war have added tens of billions to G7 debt financing costs. No specific country-by-country breakdown in what's available, but the direction is clear: governments that were already running tight fiscal positions are facing structurally higher interest bills. For UK-focused positioning, this reinforces the case that the OBR's next forecast update will be uncomfortable, and that the Chancellor has less room than the spring statement implied.

On the Iran situation, Khamenei has called on Gulf states to align against what he described as their "real enemy" while the Strait of Hormuz remains restricted and diplomacy is stalled. Al Jazeera's longer piece on the six months since the US-Israeli strikes frames the conflict as having shifted into an economic attrition phase centred on Hormuz. The combination of that and the yield story above is the macro thread that matters most heading into the week.

Iceland has voted against resuming EU accession talks, with 52.8% opposed. Not a market mover in itself, but it's a data point in the broader picture of EU enlargement fatigue and the limits of Brussels' pull in the current geopolitical climate.

Sony Music and Warner have sued Anthropic, alleging systematic copyright infringement — described in the filings as a "brazen campaign" of IP theft. This is broader in scope than previous music-AI cases and names specific piracy accusations rather than just training data disputes. It adds to the legal overhang on frontier AI companies and is worth tracking if you have exposure to Anthropic's valuation or to the broader AI infrastructure trade.

The US has struck an oil deal covering roughly a fifth of Venezuela's output, and it's being criticised from both ends of Venezuelan politics — hardliners call it a sellout, the opposition calls it Washington overreach. The Economist's read is that it gives both governments reason to delay any democratic transition. Relevant for anyone watching EM oil supply and Latin America political risk.

The Nepal-Tibet floods have killed at least 750 people with over 3,000 still missing. China is censoring domestic coverage of the Tibet side of the disaster. Not a direct market story, but the scale and the information blackout are worth noting for anyone tracking China's internal stability signals.

UK PMI data for August is due Monday morning.
