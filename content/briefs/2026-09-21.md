Brent crude has dropped to just above $100 a barrel, driven by speculation that Trump could meet the Iranian president this week. That's given a lift to French and Italian government bonds, which had been under pressure. The implied logic is a potential easing of sanctions pressure on Iranian supply, though nothing is confirmed. Worth watching whether the oil move holds or reverses on any clarification from Washington.

On Ukraine, Trump reportedly told Zelenskyy in a phone call to stop striking Russian oil refineries. The framing from officials was blunt — Trump described it as being about "diesel, diesel, diesel." It's a significant constraint on Ukrainian strategy if it holds, and it signals continued US pressure on Kyiv to limit escalation in ways that affect energy markets.

Germany's state elections over the weekend were ugly for Merz. His CDU took heavy losses in both Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Berlin, with Merz himself calling it a disaster. The Economist's framing is that German politics is fragmenting and radicalising. Merz says he'll stay on, but this weakens his domestic position ahead of any further EU-level negotiations and complicates the coalition arithmetic in Berlin.

Iran's official GDP data, cited by Al Jazeera, shows the oil and gas sector contracted 26% during the period of US-Israel military pressure, dragging overall GDP down around 10%. That's a harder number than most external estimates had suggested and gives some context to why a diplomatic opening — if one is genuinely in play — would matter economically to Tehran.

On AI, TechCrunch has a piece noting that companies working on so-called world models are being unusually secretive — founders, investors, and even data suppliers are declining to explain what they're actually building, despite significant funding. It's not a single company story, but it's a pattern worth tracking given how much capital is flowing into the space on the basis of very little disclosed technical detail.

Separately, Ars Technica reports that Google's threat intelligence group had an undercover analyst embedded inside TeamPCP, a supply-chain hacking operation. The group targets software build pipelines. No major named victim disclosed, but the story confirms Google is running active human intelligence operations against cybercriminal networks, which is a notable escalation in approach.

The Anthropic IPO valuation piece in the FT is worth a read if you have time — the maths are genuinely difficult to square at current implied multiples, and the Lex desk flags several competing frameworks without landing cleanly on any of them.

The Federal Reserve's Raphael Bostic speaks tomorrow, Tuesday 22nd September, and will be closely watched for any signal on the pace of cuts into year-end.
