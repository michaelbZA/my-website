The Burnham government's first weekend in office has generated more signal than noise. Speaking to the BBC, the Prime Minister ruled out an early general election, saying he would honour the manifesto he was elected on. On Trump, he struck a careful but not supine tone — saying he had a good first conversation with the US president but that he was prepared to call him out "if right for Britain." That framing will matter for how markets read the UK-US relationship over the coming months, particularly on trade.

Reform UK is back in the news for the wrong reasons. Police are investigating donations made by Britain Means Business — a company owned by the party's deputy leader Richard Tice — to Reform ahead of the 2024 general election. Two tranches of £250,000 each. The investigation is at an early stage, but it keeps the party's finances under scrutiny at a moment when it is trying to consolidate as a serious opposition force.

The European wildfires are now a macro story, not just a humanitarian one. More than 340,000 people have been evacuated in France and Spain, with Bordeaux city authorities actively modelling scenarios including full evacuation of the city. The disruption to southern European infrastructure, tourism revenue, and insurance exposure is significant. Reinsurers will be watching closely; this is already one of the largest peacetime civilian evacuations France has conducted.

China is accelerating green energy deal-making under Belt and Road, explicitly taking advantage of the drop in oil demand caused by the continuing Iran conflict. Beijing is positioning itself as the primary capital provider for renewables in markets where Western appetite has softened. Worth watching for how this reshapes sovereign debt exposure in emerging markets.

On AI and labour, Monday.com has become the latest tech firm to cite AI as a direct factor in layoffs — TechCrunch is now tracking more than twenty companies that have done so this year. Separately, the FT has a piece on white-collar workers expressing nostalgia for pre-AI working life, citing concerns about errors and loss of creative ownership. Neither story is new in isolation, but together they suggest the productivity narrative is meeting real workforce friction that will eventually surface in wage and hiring data.

The Berlin Pride van attack — one dead, sixteen injured, suspect still at large — is a domestic German security story for now, but the political fallout will be worth tracking given the timing ahead of Germany's autumn legislative calendar.

The UK's Q2 GDP preliminary estimate is due on Friday 31st July.
