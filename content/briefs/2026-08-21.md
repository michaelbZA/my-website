The UK ran a £1.8bn deficit in July, against City expectations of roughly zero. July is normally a strong month for the Treasury because self-assessment receipts flood in, so missing that badly is notable. Total public debt is now £2.98tn, or 94% of GDP, up £96bn year-on-year. For Healey, who is drawing up his first budget, this narrows the fiscal room he has to work with and makes any spending commitments harder to defend without either tax rises or revised borrowing rules.

Scott Bessent is making a high-stakes move in the Treasury market, shifting issuance toward longer-dated debt in an attempt to bear down on long-end yields. Wall Street's read, per FT reporting, is that it's a "band-aid on a bullet hole" — the underlying deficit dynamics haven't changed, and forcing duration onto the market without addressing the structural borrowing position risks pushing yields higher rather than lower. Worth watching how 10- and 30-year Treasuries react into next week.

HSBC has spent $68mn cutting its most senior bankers — the largest cull of highly paid staff across European lenders since 2020. The scale of the redundancy bill signals this isn't a routine restructuring; it's a meaningful reduction in headcount at the top of the house. No detail yet on which divisions bore the brunt.

On AI, new data suggests OpenAI is gaining ground on Anthropic among enterprise users, but the more interesting finding is how readily businesses switch between providers as new models drop. That's a structural problem for both companies' revenue visibility and complicates the valuation case ahead of any IPO. Enterprise AI spend looks less sticky than the bulls assumed.

Nevada has approved permits for up to 8,000 robotaxis across Tesla, Uber and Waymo over the next 12 months. The combined scale is new — previous deployments have been city-specific and far smaller. For anyone watching autonomous vehicle commercialisation timelines, this is a meaningful regulatory green light in a large US state.

The Met Office is warning that the current El Niño pattern is shaping up to be the strongest in living memory, with 2027 now flagged as likely to be the hottest year on record. That has direct relevance for agricultural commodity pricing, energy demand forecasting, and insurers with climate exposure.

UK public sector net borrowing figures for August are due next Friday, 28th August, which will give the next read on whether July's deficit miss was a one-off or the start of a trend.
