The Iran situation is the lead this morning. Trump announced Saturday night he's standing down from further strikes, saying the "perimeters of a deal" have been agreed. The Guardian reports that Saudi crown prince MBS called Trump directly to express alarm about the strike plans, and Iran had threatened decisive retaliation against its neighbours if the US hit again. Kuwait was struck by Iranian drones on Saturday, and two ships were attacked near the Strait of Hormuz. Trump's language is conditional — he said the pause is "subject to being able to rapidly make a deal" — so this is a ceasefire in tone rather than a signed agreement. Oil traders will be watching the Hormuz situation closely; the shipping attacks are a live variable regardless of what gets agreed diplomatically.

On UK domestic politics, Andy Burnham has used his devolution agenda to make a public case for a written constitution, arguing the UK's current arrangements are too antiquated to deliver equal opportunity across regions. This is a long-held view of his but he's now framing it as something his programme actively supports rather than a background aspiration. The Economist notes that Labour's Bev Craig won in Manchester, with opposition parties struggling to land a punch. Burnham's constitutional pitch is likely to generate noise from Conservatives and some Labour backbenchers this week.

The Guardian has an investigation worth flagging on Puy du Fou, the French theme park group planning a £600 million attraction in Oxfordshire. The reporting alleges the company worked with a sanctioned Russian oligarch on Crimea resort plans for longer than previously disclosed, and subsequently courted Iran and China as partners. Given the UK's sanctions regime and the current political climate around Russian-linked capital, this one could move quickly if ministers pick it up.

On the tech side, Apple is reportedly throttling the number of AI-related vulnerability submissions it will accept from security researchers, having been overwhelmed by a wave of bug reports. The FT frames this as Apple struggling to keep pace rather than managing a routine process. For anyone thinking about enterprise exposure to iOS security, it's a flag that the AI-expanded attack surface is generating more findings than the company's triage can absorb.

Reddit's CEO is publicly questioning the value of Google's AI Overviews — the feature that summarises search results without sending users to source sites — and Ars Technica reports Reddit may be reconsidering its content licensing deal with Google. That deal was a meaningful revenue line when Reddit went public. If it unravels, it hits Reddit's numbers and raises broader questions about the economics of training data agreements across the sector.

EU home affairs ministers convene on Tuesday to discuss the Spain-Ceuta migrant crisis, with Sánchez having pushed back sharply on criticism from other member states. Watch for any movement on burden-sharing language, which has historically been where these summits either produce something or collapse into communiqué fudge.

EU home affairs ministers meet Tuesday.
