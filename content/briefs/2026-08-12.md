The UK is in the middle of an extended heat emergency. Burnham is chairing a Cobra meeting today, with temperatures forecast to hit 38C on Thursday. Wildfires are stretching emergency services and drought conditions are spreading. This is not a routine weather story — it is starting to look like the kind of infrastructure and supply-chain stress event that has macro consequences. Watch for energy demand spikes and any guidance from National Grid.

On Ukraine, two things happened overnight that pull in different directions. Zelenskyy has sent formal ceasefire proposals to Washington, which is the most concrete diplomatic movement in months. At the same time, the FT is reporting that JD Vance personally asked Kyiv to stop striking tankers at Novorossiysk — vessels collecting Kazakh oil, not Russian. Washington's concern appears to be that the campaign is disrupting Caspian energy flows that matter to US partners in Central Asia. That the US is actively lobbying Ukraine to protect a Russian port is a notable shift in how Washington is managing the relationship.

The Tata succession story is worth watching if you have India exposure. N Chandrasekaran is departing as chair of Tata Sons amid what the FT describes as a dispute with Noel Tata. Tata is the largest conglomerate in India — this is not a routine board reshuffle. Details on the nature of the dispute are thin at this stage, but any instability at the top of Tata has implications for Jaguar Land Rover, Tata Steel's UK operations, and the broader India investment thesis.

On the AI and cyber front, the FT is reporting that China-linked hackers carried out what it describes as an "autonomous" AI cyber attack on Taiwan — AI agents running simultaneous reconnaissance and intrusion operations without human direction at each step. If the characterisation holds up, this marks a qualitative shift in state-sponsored cyber capability. Relevant for anyone thinking about critical infrastructure exposure or cyber insurance pricing.

Nvidia is back in the Economist's frame this week, with reporting on its $500bn push to defend its position as its largest customers — the hyperscalers — move to develop their own silicon. The competitive dynamic is not new, but the scale of Nvidia's response suggests the company sees this as an existential threat to its data centre margins rather than a manageable erosion.

Russian drones were intercepted near a major European offshore gas installation overnight, following recent incursions into Romanian airspace and a failed attack in Germany. No damage reported, but the pattern is escalating and European energy infrastructure is clearly in scope.

US CPI for July prints tomorrow, Thursday 13th August.
