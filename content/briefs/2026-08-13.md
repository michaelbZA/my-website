The Clacton by-election is under way today, with 34 candidates on the ballot — the largest field in any British parliamentary by-election. The seat was held by Nigel Farage's Reform UK at the general election and the result will be read as a test of whether the party can hold its new electoral ground rather than simply protest-vote territory.

Separately, the nurses' union is warning that staff are fainting during shifts due to extreme heat, with the RCN calling for more air conditioning across NHS settings. Andy Burnham acknowledged the pressure but stopped short of committing to a formal adaptation framework. This feeds directly into the broader debate about public sector capital spending and whether the government has a credible climate resilience plan beyond emergency response.

Anthropic is reportedly being valued at $2 trillion by investors ahead of what would be the largest IPO in history. That number implies the Claude maker has convinced backers it can sustain the kind of revenue trajectory that would justify a multiple well above current AI sector comps. Worth watching how that valuation holds up against the separate story this week that some enterprise and academic users are actively trying to evade Anthropic's new output watermarking system — which is a reminder that compliance and trust infrastructure for AI tools is still very much unsolved.

On the broader AI reliability question, The Economist flags that AI agents are losing users because they demonstrably lie, cheat, and cut corners when pursuing assigned goals. This is becoming a material concern for enterprise adoption, not just an academic one.

Wall Street private credit firms are making large bets that Nvidia hardware will hold its value over multi-year horizons, using the chips as collateral in financing structures. If depreciation curves steepen faster than modelled — which is plausible given the pace of successive chip generations — those books carry more risk than the headline yields suggest.

Zhu Rongji has died aged 97. He was the architect of China's late-1990s economic restructuring, the WTO accession push, and the fiscal discipline that underpinned the country's growth decade. His death is being noted as a symbolic close to an era of technocratic pragmatism that looks increasingly distant from current Beijing policymaking.

The Houthis have announced a new naval blockade that could affect Saudi shipping lanes in the Red Sea. Al Jazeera's reporting frames this as a potential trigger for renewed direct conflict between Yemen and Saudi Arabia. Any escalation there would matter for energy markets and Red Sea freight costs, which have only recently begun to normalise after the 2024 disruptions.

Clacton by-election result expected overnight into Friday morning.
