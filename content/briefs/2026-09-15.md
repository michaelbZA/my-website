UK wage growth came in at 3.9% for the three months to July, the slowest pace in over two years. That's the number the Bank of England has been waiting to see move, and it does shift the calculus slightly on the timing of the next rate cut. The triple lock means the state pension will now rise to just over £13,000 a year in April — the debate about affordability is getting louder, but the political will to touch the formula remains essentially zero.

Separately, the government is looking at tightening residency rules for political donors. The proximate cause is Reform UK receiving £72 million from two crypto-billionaires who've both lived abroad. Expect this to move slowly through consultation, but it's worth watching if you have any interest in how the political funding landscape shifts ahead of the next cycle.

China's domestic economy is flashing amber again. Fixed asset investment is slumping and the data released overnight adds to pressure on Beijing to announce another stimulus package. The pattern here is familiar — weak data, market expectations of policy response, partial delivery — but the deterioration in investment figures is sharper than consensus expected, and it matters for anyone with exposure to commodities or EM.

Beijing also passed a sweeping new law tightening control over overseas travel, framed around protecting state secrets and preventing the exit of skilled workers and advanced technology. It's the kind of structural measure that tends to get underweighted in the short term and overweighted in retrospect. For anyone thinking about supply chain exposure or talent mobility in China-linked businesses, it's worth a proper read.

On the geopolitical side, Russia fired flares at a Danish military helicopter in the Baltic, and separately a drone was shot down by NATO jets over Lithuanian airspace overnight. Denmark's prime minister called the behaviour reckless. Ukraine's foreign minister backed Copenhagen publicly. These incidents are escalating in frequency and the EU briefings are now explicitly about strengthening the eastern flank. Not a crisis yet, but the pattern is shifting.

Trump told reporters that AI safety concerns are a "hoax" and rejected calls for greater safeguards, including the mandatory kill-switch proposal that Anthropic's Jack Clark put to the BBC. The same day, Nvidia's Jensen Huang told Trump directly that he won't let an AI slowdown happen. The regulatory picture in the US is now clearly set: acceleration is the policy, and anyone pricing in federal guardrails in the near term should revisit that assumption.

The Thirlwall Inquiry into the Lucy Letby murders publishes its findings today. Primarily a public health governance story, but the expected findings on institutional failure to act on doctors' warnings will land hard and could have implications for NHS leadership accountability frameworks.

ONS publishes UK CPI for August tomorrow morning.
