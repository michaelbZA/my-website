The Reform UK donation story is worth flagging. The £72m from crypto billionaires Ben Delo and Christopher Harborne — the largest declared donations to any UK party since reporting requirements began in 2001 — is still generating internal friction, with activists pushing back on Farage's plan to deploy the money on professional campaign staff in target constituencies. The legality question hasn't been resolved, and that alone keeps it live as a political risk story heading into the week.

Jim Ratcliffe told the BBC he has lost confidence in the UK, describing the country as "on the slide" and calling for more North Sea investment. Coming from the founder of Ineos — a major industrial employer with significant UK operations — it's the kind of business sentiment signal that tends to get picked up in policy circles, even if it's not new ground from him.

On global macro, the US-Denmark-Greenland security deal was signed on the sidelines of UNGA. Trump is claiming "permanent control" over Greenland's security; Denmark and Greenland say sovereignty and self-determination are preserved. The gap between those two characterisations is wide enough to matter. Watch for whether the formal text, due to be signed at UNGA next week, narrows it.

Mark Carney is mid-way through a European trip aimed at redirecting Canadian trade and investment flows away from US dependency. The Economist frames it as part of a broader structural pivot. For anyone watching transatlantic capital flows or UK-Canada trade positioning post-Trump tariffs, it's worth tracking.

The FT reports Anthropic could struggle to sustain revenues post-IPO, citing OpenAI's resurgence, cheaper competitors, and ongoing safety-related reputational drag. Separately, OpenAI itself is projecting it will burn through $280bn by 2030, with deeply negative cash flows driven by infrastructure spend and pricing pressure. Both stories together sketch a sector where the revenue models remain genuinely unproven at scale — relevant for anyone with exposure to AI-linked equities or venture positions.

The Ars Technica report that an AI hallucination of Chinese nuclear components nearly triggered a US military strike is the most consequential AI-risk story of the week and deserves more attention than it's getting. The piece notes the military's overall AI adoption is accelerating regardless.

The FCC has approved Paramount selling a 49.5% equity stake to sovereign investors from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar, rejecting concerns about repressive-government influence over CBS. That's a meaningful media ownership shift with geopolitical dimensions that will likely resurface in Congressional debate.

UNGA convenes in New York on Monday, with the Greenland deal signing and Palestinian President Abbas's pre-recorded address both scheduled.
