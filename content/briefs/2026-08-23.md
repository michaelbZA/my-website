The Canada-US trade situation has escalated sharply. Mark Carney announced retaliatory tariffs over the weekend, describing the situation as a trade war and saying Trump had "miscalculated" by pushing further. Canadian provincial premiers have piled on, and the language from Ottawa is now notably harder than it was even a fortnight ago. For anyone with North American exposure, this is no longer a negotiating posture story — it's a live tariff event with retaliation in place.

On UK domestic policy, mayors in England are set to receive powers to overrule local councils on planning decisions. The framing from housing minister Matthew Pennycook is explicitly about housing delivery, and this sits alongside the separate news that onshore windfarm applications in England hit a ten-year high in the year to March — about 45 submissions, following Labour's removal of the Conservative-era ban. Both stories point in the same direction: the government is trying to use planning reform as an economic lever, and the pipeline of consented projects in both housing and energy is starting to move.

The cyber attack on a small UK power plant, which occurred in July and was only confirmed publicly this weekend, is worth noting. The government says there was no risk to the wider energy system, but the fact of a successful attack on grid infrastructure — however contained — will matter to anyone thinking about critical infrastructure risk or the regulatory response that typically follows.

Israel's strike on a Syrian air base is drawing pointed commentary from Washington's own ambassador to Ankara, who suggested it may have been an attempt to provoke a conflict with Turkey ahead of Israeli elections in October. That's a striking thing for a US envoy to say on the record, and it complicates the picture for anyone tracking Middle East stability and what it means for regional energy and defence positioning.

On the tech side, British AI lab Inherent — founded by DeepMind alumni — has released an agent called Faraday, claiming it outperformed Anthropic and OpenAI on scientific paper replication benchmarks. The lab is small and the claim needs scrutiny, but DeepMind pedigree and a UK base makes this one to watch for anyone tracking where serious AI research capability is concentrating outside the US hyperscalers.

UK inflation data for July is due Monday morning.
