The Colombia earthquake is the biggest humanitarian story in the articles but doesn't move markets directly, so worth a brief mention for geopolitical context. Let me pick the stories that matter for a UK finance professional.

---

Hong Kong is moving to include trading firms in its forthcoming tax reform package, part of a broader effort to compete with Singapore for financial talent. The proposal extends preferential treatment beyond asset managers to proprietary trading houses, which would be a meaningful shift in the territory's competitive positioning. Worth watching if you have views on Asia Pacific financial centre dynamics.

The EU's new border check regime is causing peak waiting times of two hours at Frankfurt and Amsterdam, according to the FT. That's a logistical drag on European business travel and freight flows, and it's feeding into a broader political row: Sweden's PM Ulf Kristersson has publicly called Spain's migrant amnesty a "very bad idea," warning it threatens the integrity of Schengen. The combination of tightening border controls and political friction over free movement is another slow-burn headwind for European integration narratives.

Meta is pushing out new open-weight AI models in what Zuckerberg is framing as a strategic reset. The company has been losing ground to OpenAI and Google on frontier models, and the pivot toward open-source is partly a competitive play and partly a bid to build ecosystem lock-in through developer adoption. Separately, OpenAI has completed a $7 billion employee tender offer, which tidies up its cap table ahead of any eventual public market event. Also from OpenAI: the company has launched a dedicated cybersecurity AI model under its Daybreak programme, positioning itself in a market where AI-led attacks are accelerating. That's relevant for anyone with exposure to legacy cybersecurity names.

The FT has a substantive piece on AI's threat to India's IT services sector. The argument is straightforward: the model of labour arbitrage that built Infosys, Wipro, and TCS is structurally vulnerable to automation, and the disruption timeline is compressing. If you hold Indian IT at index weight or above, this is worth reading in full.

Colombia's 7.4 magnitude earthquake killed at least 132 people, with buildings collapsed across Cali and Pereira. No direct market read, but it adds pressure to an already stressed Andean region and may affect infrastructure and insurance exposures.

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced that councils will receive new powers to block vape shops and betting shops from opening in town centres. Modest policy, but it signals continued regulatory pressure on the consumer finance and retail sectors at local level.

UK CPI data for July is due Wednesday morning.
