The Reform party has received a £36 million donation from Ben Delo, a crypto billionaire, making it the largest single donation ever given to a UK political party. The timing is notable — Reform has been slipping in the polls as Labour and the Conservatives rediscover a more conventional left-right dividing line. A war chest of that size changes the electoral arithmetic heading into the local and mayoral cycle, regardless of where the headline numbers sit today.

Saudi Arabia has shut the East-West pipeline following a drone attack launched from Iraqi territory near the Iranian border. That pipeline is the kingdom's main route for bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, so closing it is not a minor operational inconvenience — it removes the principal alternative to Hormuz just as the FT reports the US has restricted air defence time slots for tankers transiting the strait to two specified windows daily, a response to an uptick in Iranian attacks. Oil prices were already moving before this; both stories together will push them further. Watch Brent on Monday open.

On the geopolitical backdrop to that: Trump visited Dublin and made comments suggesting he would welcome a united Ireland, telling reporters "it may as well happen now." He also said the Iran war would end "very soon." Neither statement came with detail, but the Ireland comment in particular will land awkwardly in Westminster and will need a response from Starmer before the week is out.

The EU is moving to lock in budget and climate measures before France's presidential election in April, with Paris itself apparently urging speed. The logic is straightforward — if Le Pen wins, the current consensus on both files collapses. Diplomats are treating April as a hard deadline.

Twenty-five mathematicians, including Fields Medal winners, have signed an open letter accusing OpenAI of threatening their intellectual work — the dispute centres on AI labs training on mathematical research without consent or credit. The FT separately ran a long piece on existential risk fears becoming mainstream inside the labs themselves, driven by progress on autonomous agents and the intensifying rivalry between OpenAI and Anthropic. Neither story is directly tradeable this week, but both feed into the regulatory environment that will shape AI legislation in Westminster and Brussels through the autumn.

UK GDP and trade data for August are due Monday morning.
