The Clacton by-election result is in. Farage won with 22,239 votes, a comfortable margin over a field of 34 candidates that every major party boycotted. The Economist's read is that it's a Pyrrhic victory — he's back in parliament but Reform's financial and organisational problems haven't gone away. For anyone watching the party as a political force rather than a polling phenomenon, the question remains whether he can build a machine or is just running a personal brand.

OpenAI and Anthropic are in an open price war, cutting model costs as Chinese AI competitors close the gap. The FT frames this as a direct response to pressure on their trillion-dollar valuations. Separately, Chinese tech is having a moment — the Star 50 index is up 29% this year, with Beijing's support amplifying the AI frenzy into valuation multiples that now exceed US peers on some metrics. Worth watching if you have any view on the US-China tech divergence trade.

JPMorgan quietly debanked Polymarket over regulatory concerns, even as it has continued cultivating ties with the prediction market platform, which is targeting a $20bn valuation. The tension between those two facts tells you something about where banks think the regulatory wind is blowing on crypto-adjacent platforms.

A Trump administration memo has, for the first time, authorised private sector firms to conduct offensive cyberattacks against overseas criminals. The practical and legal boundaries of that are entirely unclear, but it's a meaningful policy shift for anyone with exposure to cybersecurity or critical infrastructure.

The NHS data breach is worth a note — transplant patient records were transmitted over an unencrypted pager network. Small story in isolation, but it adds to the pattern of NHS digital infrastructure failures that will eventually force a capital commitment from the Treasury.

US GDP data for Q2 is due for revision today, alongside UK retail sales figures for July.
