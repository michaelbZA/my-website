UK inflation data is due Wednesday, and it matters. Economists are forecasting headline CPI at 2.9% for July, driven almost entirely by the surge in household energy bills. That would put inflation back close to 3% and frame the first major policy test for whoever emerges as the new Prime Minister. The Iran war's continued pressure on global energy markets is the proximate cause, and with gas and electricity bills having risen sharply last month, the Bank of England will find it harder to justify further rate cuts in the near term. Worth watching whether the services component stays sticky — that's what the MPC will be focused on.

On the US political picture, the FT is reporting poll findings showing a majority of American voters say they are worse off under Trump, with Democrats now leading Republicans on economic management ahead of November's midterms. That's a meaningful shift. Combined with the Guardian's reporting that Israel flatly rejected Trump's latest Gaza peace proposals days after he declared a "historic breakthrough," the administration looks politically exposed on multiple fronts simultaneously. A Republican midterm rout would complicate the second-term legislative agenda and shift the calculus on trade and fiscal policy heading into 2027.

Ukraine struck a major Russian rocket manufacturing facility over the weekend using cruise missiles. Separately, a NATO member — Romania — shot down a drone over its territory, though Bucharest hasn't formally attributed it to Russia. The pattern of incidents on NATO's eastern flank is becoming harder to dismiss as isolated. No Article 5 trigger, but the pressure on alliance unity is building.

SpaceX has formally closed its acquisition of Cursor, the AI coding assistant. The deal puts one of the most widely used developer tools inside Elon Musk's industrial empire, which already spans satellite infrastructure, defence contracting, and now a significant slice of the AI software stack used by engineers globally. The strategic concentration is worth noting regardless of your view on Musk.

Singapore is explicitly positioning its dual access to US and Chinese AI models as a competitive advantage over Hong Kong in the race to retain finance talent. If that framing takes hold, it has implications for where institutions choose to domicile Asia-Pacific operations — and for how the AI access divide between jurisdictions starts to affect capital flows.

UK CPI for July publishes Wednesday morning.
