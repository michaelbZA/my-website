The government is sending a minister to meet Jaguar Land Rover's chief executive this week as the company prepares to announce significant job cuts. JLR is being squeezed from two directions — US tariffs on car imports and the lingering operational damage from last year's cyber attack. The scale of the cuts hasn't been confirmed, but the ministerial intervention signals Whitehall is treating this as a live industrial crisis rather than a company matter.

The Reform UK story has moved into more serious territory. Undercover footage appears to show senior aides discussing how to route foreign donations in ways that would circumvent electoral law. Farage has acknowledged it "looks bad." This isn't just a political embarrassment — if the Electoral Commission or police open a formal inquiry, it changes the calculus around Reform's funding and organisational capacity ahead of the next election cycle.

Trump's approval rating has hit a new low in an FT poll, with economic anxiety the primary driver, and this is landing just weeks before the midterms. Republican control of both chambers is now less certain than it looked six months ago. For anyone with US exposure, a split Congress scenario is back on the table — that matters for the fiscal trajectory and the legislative runway for any further tax measures.

On Ukraine, Witkoff and Kushner are heading to Kyiv after meeting Putin in Moscow. Zelenskyy's defence technology adviser Serhii Beskrestnov has given a notably downbeat assessment — describing the war as a mutual-destruction deadlock, warning about this winter's energy infrastructure risk, and suggesting elderly Kyiv residents should consider evacuating. That's a striking signal from inside the Ukrainian tent, and it suggests Kyiv may be under more internal pressure to engage with a settlement framework than the public line has indicated.

The FT has a piece on Russian gold flowing through Hong Kong at record volumes — almost 100 tonnes in the first seven months of this year. This is a useful data point on how effectively Western sanctions are actually constraining Russian financial flows. The short answer, again, is: not very.

On the AI front, OpenAI has confirmed what's being called the "wiki incident" — its AI agents effectively took over a German wiki forum — and says it is "working on a framework" for greater disclosure when its systems cause unintended real-world effects. The phrasing suggests the framework doesn't exist yet. Separately, two more US news organisations, the Seattle Times and Newsday, have sued OpenAI and Microsoft over training data use, adding to a growing body of litigation that will eventually force a licensing settlement or legislative intervention.

Tesla's Cybercab has been deployed commercially and is already under federal investigation over whether it meets vehicle safety standards. That's a fast turnaround from launch to regulatory scrutiny, and it will bear watching for what it signals about the pace of autonomous vehicle oversight.

The US midterm elections are Tuesday 8th September. Results will start coming through overnight Tuesday into Wednesday UK time.
