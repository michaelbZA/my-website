UK inflation jumped to 2.9% in July, up from 2.6% in June, driven by higher energy costs linked to the Iran war. The ONS data lands awkwardly for the Burnham government, which has been talking up relief for households, and it complicates the Bank of England's position. The jobs market is softening, so the MPC is caught between an inflation overshoot and weakening demand. Expect the rate path debate to get noisier this week.

On the trade front, Trump has reached a last-minute deal with Canada, pausing the threatened 50% tariffs following what both sides described as intense talks with Mark Carney. The reprieve is real but fragile — no detail yet on what Canada conceded or how durable the arrangement is. For anyone with North American exposure, this reduces the immediate tail risk but doesn't resolve the underlying tension.

The UAE has suspended trade with Iran after saying Iranian missiles were fired at the Gulf state, targeting maritime traffic before falling into the sea. Tehran has separately told the FT that it is drawing up proposals to strike US military assets in Europe if the conflict escalates further. Both developments point to the Iran war broadening beyond the Middle East faster than markets have priced. Energy and shipping routes are the direct exposure.

On the interceptor missile shortage, the Economist flags that Western stockpiles of air defence missiles are running critically low, with Ukraine and allied governments increasingly alarmed. This has direct implications for defence procurement budgets across NATO members and is worth watching for UK defence spending signals.

The Meta antitrust trial is drawing comparisons to big tobacco litigation in scale and strategy, though the Economist notes investors are not yet treating it as an existential threat. Worth monitoring given the potential structural implications for digital advertising markets if the court finds against Meta on its acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp.

OpenAI has introduced new model monitoring and alignment safeguards following a breach at Hugging Face. The details are thin, but the timing — ahead of what is expected to be a significant product cycle — suggests the company is managing reputational risk as much as technical risk.

UK CPI for July is already out, but the next significant scheduled event is the Bank of England's Huw Pill speaking on Thursday morning.
